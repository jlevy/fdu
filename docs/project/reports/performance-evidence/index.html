Making fdu Faster, One Measured Experiment at a Time

fdu is a disk-usage and file roll-up tool written in Rust. Its performance work is a loop of experiments, each testing one change against the code it came from, and a change is kept only if it passes the accept rule. The record holds 200 experiments, including 61 rejected changes.

The Loop

An experiment names a hypothesis and a predicted effect, builds exactly one change, checks that every answer is unchanged, and times the change (the candidate) against the code it came from (the control): its two arms. Both binaries alternate in one interleaved session, a cell, on a tree pinned by content digest, for at least 12 rounds, or 20 when the predicted effect is small; a round is one paired trial of each arm (the harness’s --trials). The paired change is the median over the rounds of the candidate’s change from the control in the same round, so drift in the host’s speed reaches both arms alike; its 95% interval is a bootstrap of that median. A change is kept when it is at least 3% faster with its 95% interval below zero. Some are accepted instead on noninferiority, when the interval rules out a slowdown beyond a stated margin.

The harness reads host CPU load before and after every sample. On a quiet host a sample with a reading above 25% busy, the quiet gate, is discarded; an uncontrolled host keeps every sample and records the load. A score is exploratory, for discovery rather than for quoting, when any cell behind it ran on an uncontrolled host, ran fewer than 20 rounds, or belongs to an exploratory stage (host-pressure regimes).

Relative

What each experiment did

Milliseconds say how fast the tool is, but not whether a particular change made it so, because the host's speed drifts: the peer tool dust, whose binary never changed, measured 210 ms to 327 ms on the same tree across 9 runs. Every experiment therefore interleaved its two builds and compared them in pairs, and each verdict rests on the paired change below.

A change that looks promising often measures within a few percent of zero, which is why a speed-up is accepted only when it clears the threshold with its interval below zero. The 61 rejections are the reusable part of the record: each is an idea the next person need not try again.

Scale

Does it hold on a bigger tree, and on another kernel?

The record measured 53 fingerprinted states of 32 trees (subject families), from 307 to 1,009,679 entries, on macOS and Linux. Their milliseconds are not comparable. Cost per entry is closer to comparable, since a scan's work grows roughly linearly with the entries it visits.

Reading These Numbers

The two figures do not divide into each other

Dividing a row's endpoints in the absolute figure does not give the relative figure's percentage. The absolute values are each arm's median on its own; the relative value is the median of the paired changes, the candidate against the control within each round. When the host drifts during a run the two diverge, in this record by several percentage points and sometimes in sign.

exp‑005's cold-scan-index reads +2.8% from its medians and −3.9% paired. The paired figure controls for drift, so verdicts use it. The page publishes both, and neither is derived from the other.

Accepted and faster are different questions

15 experiments measured an improvement and were not accepted, for reasons such as falling below the threshold, being superseded, or being unfinished. 40 were accepted without a measured improvement on their primary metric, on grounds such as noninferiority, instrumentation, or correctness evidence.

Instrumentation is the clearest case: exp‑052 and exp‑053 were accepted on intervals of [−3.3%, +3.8%] and [−3.0%, +1.4%]. Neither was claimed as a speed-up; each made the engine observable at a cost the measurement could not detect.

What is not measured here

Every number comes from one Apple M1 Pro or a handful of virtualized 4-vCPU Linux guests; nothing is from bare-metal Linux, and Windows is not benchmarked. The page cache was warm throughout because dropping it needs root, so nothing here describes a cold disk. Tuning constants were fitted on the subjects shown.
